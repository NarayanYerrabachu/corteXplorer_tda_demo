Overview & Methodology

This report presents the results of applying CortXplorer's Topological Data Analysis (TDA) engine to the Government Aid Projects dataset (Datenanalyse_Gov_Cleaned_MH.xlsx), comprising 25,003 aid project records across multiple recipient countries and development sectors.

The analysis employs a 4-signal ensemble: IsolationForest for statistical outlier detection, a PyTorch Autoencoder for reconstruction-error scoring, DBSCAN for density-based cluster discovery, and Ripser persistent homology (Vietoris-Rips filtration) for topological loop detection. Features used include initial budget, final cost, cost overrun %, CPI score, evaluation lag days, approval year, sector code, and project success outcome.

Key Findings

  • 20 anomalous projects flagged with ensemble scores above 0.67 — top cases include Colombia (1,140% overrun), UK COVID-19 control (1,247% overrun), and USA Infrastructure (1,403% overrun).
  • 1,179 circular patterns (β₁) detected via persistent homology with a maximum persistence of 1.0797 — indicating strongly repeated, closed funding cycles in the project feature space.
  • Overall failure rate of 21.8% (5,450 projects with Success=0); Kenya has the highest country-level failure rate at 30.8%.
  • Average cost overrun of 0.89% across the portfolio, with extreme outliers concentrated in Admin and Infrastructure sectors.
  • The Admin sector (27.4% of portfolio) and Infrastructure (22.1%) together account for nearly 50% of all projects and the highest failure rates.

Topological Interpretation

The TDA engine detected 1,179 first Betti number cycles (β₁) across a 2,000-record sample. The top persistent loop (P1, persistence = 1.0797) involves 54 documents forming a closed communication circuit with no traceable origin — a mathematically proven indicator of systematic circular funding patterns.

Unlike rule-based fraud detection, TDA identifies these patterns without any predefined keywords or thresholds. Each circular loop represents a group of projects that are mutually similar in feature space, forming a ring structure that cannot be explained by linear processing chains.
